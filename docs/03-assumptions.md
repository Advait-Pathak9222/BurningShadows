## The reviewer hour

`reviewer_cost_per_hour_inr: 1200` is the single most load-bearing assumption in the business
case, because every attention figure and the whole "people are most of the bill" argument scales
with it. It was **derived, not measured**, and it sits at the **top** of a defensible range, which
is the end that flatters our own argument. That is worth stating plainly.

### How it is built

A loaded hour is `base salary x loaded multiplier / productive hours`. None of the three inputs is
ours.

| Input | Value used | External anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Base salary | INR 10,00,000 | Content moderator averages in India cluster around INR 3.3L to 5.5L. A trust and safety analyst competent to judge a payment-route answer sits well above that. |
| Loaded multiplier | 1.8x | Published fully loaded multipliers run 1.25x to 1.4x for standard benefits and up to 1.8x once facilities, management, tooling, recruiting and attrition are counted. |
| Productive hours | 1,500 a year | 2,080 gross, less leave, training, breaks and time off the queue. |

`10,00,000 x 1.8 / 1,500 = INR 1,200`. At six minutes a case that is **INR 120 a review**.

### The band, and why the conclusion survives it

| Profile | Base | Loaded | Hours | Per hour | Per review | Review to check |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Moderator, low | 3,92,393 | 1.4x | 1,700 | 323 | 32 | 9x |
| Trust and safety analyst | 6,00,000 | 1.6x | 1,560 | 615 | 62 | 19x |
| Senior analyst | 8,00,000 | 1.7x | 1,500 | 907 | 91 | 28x |
| **Specialist, shipped value** | **10,00,000** | **1.8x** | **1,500** | **1,200** | **120** | **38x** |

Attention as a share of total assurance cost, across that band:

| Compute budget | INR 300/hr | INR 600/hr | INR 900/hr | INR 1,200/hr | INR 1,800/hr |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10% | 91.2% | 95.4% | 96.9% | **97.6%** | 98.4% |
| 25% | 81.9% | 90.1% | 93.2% | **94.8%** | 96.5% |
| 100% | 50.9% | 67.5% | 75.7% | **80.6%** | 86.2% |

**The claim to make is the conservative one.** At the budgets an enterprise would actually run,
which is where the knee sits at ten to twenty five percent, human attention is **82% to 98% of the
assurance bill across the entire band**. That holds even at a low-cost moderator rate. The
81% to 98% range quoted in the README is the 1,200 row across compute budgets specifically, and is
cited as such there.

Where it does break down is at full coverage on a cheap desk. At a 100% compute budget and an
INR 300 reviewer, attention falls to 50.9% and the argument becomes a coin flip. We do not
recommend running at full coverage, and the knee curve is the reason, but the sensitivity is real
and is recorded here rather than left for someone else to find.

### What would settle it

One week of a client queue study during the pilot. Actual handling time per case and the actual
loaded rate for whoever is doing the reviewing. Both numbers replace the assumption directly in
`config/economics.yaml`, and every figure downstream moves with them.

## Token prices

The offline prototype does not call a paid model, so provider token prices are not used to produce a result. `v` is a labelled scenario assumption. A production adapter must load a dated provider price table, stamp its version in the trace, and keep input, cached-input, output, and tool pricing separate. Hard-coding a current vendor price would create a false sense of accuracy and would age quickly.

## Incident cost sensitivity

The point values in the policy packs are placeholders. Before a pilot, each route needs low/base/high consequence ranges approved by Finance and Risk. The evaluation should be rerun across those ranges. A decision that changes across plausible values must be presented as sensitive, not deterministic.

## False positives and false negatives

The repository does not assume an overall FP or FN rate. It computes intervention precision, escaped harms, and deterministic catch outcomes on the held-out synthetic set. Those values describe only the seeded templates and stubs. External datasets in `06-datasets.md` are needed for a detector study.
