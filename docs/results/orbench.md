# OR-Bench: what we do to safe traffic that looks dangerous

Endpoints fixed in [Pre-registration 10](../PREREGISTRATION.md) before any detector was run.
Regenerated by `make benchmarks`.

Every other corpus in this project asks whether we catch harm. This one asks the question an
operator actually loses sleep over: **how much safe traffic does the guardrail block?** Until now
that had never been measured, because the only negatives we had were decoys we wrote ourselves,
which are exactly as hard as we thought to make them.

Corpus: [OR-Bench](https://huggingface.co/datasets/bench-llms/or-bench), CC-BY-4.0. `or-bench-hard-1k`
is 1,319 prompts that state-of-the-art models refuse *even though they are safe*; `or-bench-toxic`
is 655 genuinely toxic prompts from the same ten categories. Category-matched, so a safe row and a
toxic row differ only in their label. Deterministic 50/50 fold by prompt hash: 997 calibration,
**977 held-out** rows at a 33.5% toxic rate. No prompt appears in both files and none crosses folds.

## Result

| | Shipped lexical Tier 1 | Fitted Tier 1 |
|---|---:|---:|
| AUC | 0.5099 | **0.7837** |
| AUPRC | 0.3414 | 0.6594 |
| F1 at a calibration-chosen threshold | 0.5015 | **0.6888** |
| flag-everything null | 0.5015 | 0.5015 |
| **False-refusal rate on safe prompts** | **1.0000** | **0.1354** |
| Toxic catch rate | 1.0000 | 0.6667 |
| Degenerate operating point? | **yes** | no |

**The shipped lexical detectors fail this benchmark completely.** AUC 0.5099 is chance, and the
threshold chosen on calibration flags **every single row**. Its F1 equals the flag-everything null
to four decimals.

That is not a surprise once you look at why, and the mechanism is worth stating: across 977 rows
Tier 0 emits **one distinct value** on `unsafe_content` and one on `bias`. Its rules match nothing in
OR-Bench's vocabulary. Tier 1 raw scores 0.4955. Our lexical rules were written against our own
corpus, and OR-Bench's negatives are adversarially selected to defeat exactly that kind of keyword
matching — which is the entire point of the benchmark.

Substituting the fitted bag-of-words Tier 1, the same substitution Pre-registration 6 made for
ToxicChat, moves AUC to **0.7837** and gives a usable operating point.

## Primary endpoint: PARTIAL

The pre-registered target was Llama-3-70b's published pair — refuse fewer than 37.7% of safe
prompts while catching at least 78.7% of toxic ones.

| | ours (fitted) | Llama-3-70b | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| False-refusal on safe prompts | **13.54%** | 37.7% | **we are better** |
| Toxic catch rate | 66.67% | 78.7% | we are worse |

**Partial**, exactly as the pre-registration defines it. We refuse a third as much safe traffic and
catch about 12 points less harm.

### Against the full published set, honestly

| Model | Safe rejection | Toxic rejection |
|---|---:|---:|
| Claude-3-Opus | 91.0% | 98.1% |
| Llama-3-70b | 37.7% | 78.7% |
| GPT-4o | 6.7% | 84.9% |
| Mistral-large | 9.7% | 72.8% |
| **ControlPlane (fitted Tier 1)** | **13.5%** | **66.7%** |

We are not on the frontier. **GPT-4o and Mistral-large dominate us on both axes** — they refuse less
safe traffic *and* catch more toxic traffic. Claude-3-Opus and Llama-3-70b do not dominate us; they
buy their higher catch rates with far more over-refusal.

The published finding this benchmark is built on is that safe-rejection and toxic-rejection correlate
at Spearman **0.878** across models — almost everyone trades one for the other. Our point sits in the
low-over-refusal region of that trade-off rather than off the curve entirely.

### The matched-recall comparison does not work, and why

The fair way to compare an adjustable-threshold system against fixed-operating-point models is to
match one axis and read off the other. We cannot: **at every published catch rate, reaching it
requires flagging everything.** Between recall 0.667 (false-refusal 0.135) and recall 0.728 the score
has a cliff with no threshold inside it.

That is a resolution limit, not a detection limit, and it was worth chasing down. The raw fitted
detector reaches recall 0.787 at a false-positive rate of **0.2323** — comfortably better than
Llama-3-70b's 37.7%. The signal exists; the shipped score cannot be tuned to it, for two reasons:

1. **Isotonic calibration was piecewise-constant.** With 95 positives in a 331-row fitting fold, most
   blocks are singletons whose mean is exactly 0.0 or 1.0, so 977 rows collapsed onto **five**
   distinct scores. Interpolating between block boundaries instead — now shipped, and it costs
   nothing in calibration quality (ECE 0.0291 to 0.0292 on the synthetic corpus) — raises that to 94
   and lifts AUPRC from 0.6442 to 0.6594.
2. **Combining with a Tier 0 that has no signal here costs 0.05 AUC.** Fitted Tier 1 alone scores
   0.8537; the noisy-or combination scores 0.8053. The combination rule assumes each tier contributes
   evidence, and a tier with no coverage of the domain contributes noise instead.

The second is unfixed and is the most concrete open defect this benchmark surfaced.

Two things the 13.5% pooled figure conceals:

- **`deception` at 46.7% false-refusal is worse than Llama-3-70b's overall rate.** Prompts about
  spotting manipulation, writing fiction involving a con, or explaining a scam read as instructions
  for one.
- **`hate` catch is 22.6%.** We miss more than three quarters of genuinely hateful prompts while
  refusing 18.5% of the safe ones in the same category — the worst cell in the table, and the one an
  adversary would find first.

## The guarantee is vacuous here

Released unchecked: **0**. Mandatory coverage: **1.0000**. The bound holds and means nothing.

With a 33.5% base rate against α = 0.15, the floor obliges a check on essentially every row. As on
Aegis, the general rule is that **the release floor is informative only when α exceeds the harm base
rate**. This is recorded as vacuous and is not counted among the corpora on which the guarantee held.

## Allocation

The allocator wins 3 of 7 budgets, with gains from 0.00% to +1.16%. Below the 5.6250% blanket-Tier-1
boundary the floor already obliges ₹175.86 of the ₹176 that blanket coverage costs, so there is
nothing to allocate; above it, blanket coverage is affordable and nothing beats it. Same two
conditions as everywhere else, same verdict.

## What this corpus changed

- **The over-refusal question now has a number**: 13.5% on adversarially-hard safe prompts, against
  6.7%–91.0% for published models.
- It found the isotonic calibration resolution defect, which was costing ranking on every corpus.
- It showed the shipped lexical Tier 0/Tier 1 have no coverage outside the vocabulary they were
  written against — a disclaimer the README already carried, now a measurement.
- Two per-category failures that no pooled score would have shown.
