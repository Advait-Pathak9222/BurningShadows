# Aegis 1.0: the multi-axis test, and a failed endpoint

Endpoints fixed in [Pre-registration 9](../PREREGISTRATION.md) before any detector was run.
Regenerated by `make benchmarks`.

**The primary endpoint failed.** That is reported first because it is the result.

Corpus: [NVIDIA Aegis AI Content Safety Dataset 1.0](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Aegis-AI-Content-Safety-Dataset-1.0),
10,798 calibration and 1,199 held-out rows, 3–4 trained annotators each, 13 risk categories.
**CC-BY-4.0** — the only corpus here besides RAGTruth without a non-commercial clause, and the only
one that labels three of our five axes at once.

---

## Primary endpoint: FAILED

AUPRC had to land inside the published band for this benchmark, 0.860 (Perspective API) to 0.941
(Aegis-tuned Llama Guard). It did not, under either label convention.

| Condition | AUC | AUPRC | in band? |
|---|---:|---:|:--:|
| Defensive, fitted Tier 1 | 0.7366 | **0.8114** | no |
| Defensive, shipped lexical Tier 1 | 0.5293 | 0.6787 | no |
| Permissive, fitted Tier 1 | 0.7344 | 0.7262 | no |
| Permissive, shipped lexical Tier 1 | 0.5324 | 0.5551 | no |

Our best is **0.8114 against a floor of 0.860** — below Perspective API, the weakest published
system on this benchmark. Both of NVIDIA's own conventions are shown because both are published;
neither rescues the result.

### The F1 column is a trap, and it is ours

An earlier draft of this page led with F1, where we look strong: 0.8093 defensive, against 0.62
published for Llama Guard Base and 0.85 for Aegis Defensive.

**That number is almost entirely the base rate.** At Aegis's 66% harmful rate, a policy that flags
every single row scores F1 = 2p/(1+p) = **0.7956**. Our shipped lexical detectors flagged **100% of
rows** and scored 0.7956 — the null to four decimals. The fitted detector reaches 0.8093, which is
**+0.014 over flagging everything**.

| Condition | F1 | flag-everything null | flag rate | degenerate? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|:--:|
| Defensive, fitted | 0.8093 | 0.7956 | 0.778 | no |
| Defensive, lexical | 0.7956 | 0.7956 | **1.000** | **yes** |
| Permissive, fitted | 0.7207 | 0.6910 | 0.696 | no |
| Permissive, lexical | 0.6910 | 0.6910 | **1.000** | **yes** |

`benchmark_metrics.py` now computes that null beside every F1 and marks a flag-everything operating
point degenerate, because without it this page would have claimed to beat Llama Guard Base while
running a detector that says yes to everything. F1 on a corpus this imbalanced is not comparable
across systems and we do not claim it is.

## The train/test split leaks, and we report both ways

**131 of 1,199 test rows (10.93%) repeat a string that appears in train.** Found by a corpus
integrity check before any score was computed. Pre-registration 9 locked the shipped split, so that
stays the primary; the text-disjoint condition is reported beside it.

| Condition | AUPRC shipped | AUPRC text-disjoint | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Defensive, fitted | 0.8114 | 0.8052 | −0.0062 |
| Permissive, fitted | 0.7262 | 0.7214 | −0.0048 |

The leak is real but small, and removing it does not change the verdict. Aegis also ships 336
duplicate ids in train and 3 in test, so `interaction_id` is derived from the **text** rather than
the id — that keeps two copies of one string in the same fitting/selection fold, which is the
discipline whose loss drove a certified 0.1407 against a measured 0.2800 on ToxicChat.

## The secondary endpoint that matters most: the precondition holds

[`allocation-regime.md`](allocation-regime.md) says budget-aware allocation can only help when the
harm mix varies, measured as `Spearman(risk, expected loss) < 1`. Until now that condition had only
ever been observed **failing**: 1.000000 on ToxicChat, 0.998494 on BeaverTails.

| Corpus | Axes labelled | Spearman | Precondition |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| ToxicChat | 2 (1 effective) | 1.000000 | fails |
| BeaverTails 7% | 4 | 0.998494 | marginal |
| Synthetic | 5 | 0.941539 | holds |
| **Aegis, defensive** | **3** | **0.815965** | **holds** |
| Aegis, permissive | 3 | 0.909260 | holds |

**This is the first public corpus where the precondition is satisfied.** It is also the corpus that
shows the precondition is **necessary but not sufficient**: the allocator still wins only **1 of 7**
budgets, with gains between −1.14% and +0.10%.

The reason is the second condition, and it is visible in the economics. Blanket Tier 1 costs ₹216
against ₹3,837 for full Tier 2 — the boundary at **5.6250%**, exactly `0.18 / 3.20`, identical to
every other corpus because it is arithmetic. Below that boundary the conformal floor already obliges
₹215.82, so the floor *is* blanket coverage and there is nothing left to allocate. Above it, blanket
Tier 1 is affordable and no selective policy beats it.

**Both conditions are computable before deployment, and on this corpus they say do not bother.**

## Where the guarantee stops meaning anything

| | value |
|---|---:|
| Released unchecked | **0** |
| Mandatory coverage | **1.0000** |
| Observed unchecked-harm rate | 0.0000 |
| Holds | yes |
| **Vacuous** | **yes** |

At a 52.8–66.1% base rate against α = 0.15, the floor demands a check on essentially every row, so
the bound is satisfied by construction and carries no information. It is recorded as vacuous and
**must not be counted as another corpus on which the guarantee held**.

The general statement is worth having: **the release floor is only informative when α exceeds the
harm base rate.** Aegis at 66% and α = 0.15 is a corpus where an operator should either raise α or
accept that they are checking everything. That is a real deployment boundary, computable in advance
from two numbers.

## Per-axis: the first public measurement of `pii_leak`, and it contradicts our headline

| Axis | Positives | Lexical AUC | Fitted AUC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `bias` | 125 | 0.6294 | **0.9169** |
| `pii_leak` | 66 | **0.5834** | **0.9134** |
| `unsafe_content` | 673 | 0.5100 | 0.7774 |

Our hand-written PII mechanism scores **0.5834 here against 0.9879 on our own corpus**. That gap is
not a regression, it is the thing [`pii.md`](pii.md) argues at length: our detector scores whether a
disclosure is *authorised by its evidence*, and Aegis labels whether text *contains* private
information. Different question, and on Aegis's question our answer is close to chance. The
bag-of-words model, which is built for Aegis's question, gets 0.9134.

Pre-registration 9 predicted we would do markedly worse on this axis. **We did**, and it is the
cleanest confirmation available that the PII headline is a claim about authorisation and not about
recognition.

## Per text type

| `text_type` | n | Base rate | Fitted AUC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `llm_response` | 330 | 0.630 | **0.8025** |
| `combined` | 332 | 0.602 | 0.7956 |
| `multi_turn` | 178 | 0.848 | 0.7627 |
| `user_message` | 359 | 0.649 | **0.6747** |

We are best on model responses and worst on user messages, by 0.13 AUC. That is the expected shape —
Tier 1 reads only the response field — and it is a reason to be cautious about reading our numbers
on prompt-classification benchmarks as if they described the same system.

## What this corpus changed

- Two defects in our own measurement code, both found here: an F1 reported without its
  flag-everything null, and a degeneracy check that did not catch a detector flagging 100% of rows.
- **The allocation precondition is confirmed satisfiable on public data**, and simultaneously shown
  to be insufficient on its own.
- The `pii_leak` headline is now bounded by a public measurement of what it does *not* claim.
- One more permissively-licensed corpus, and a failed primary endpoint on the record.
